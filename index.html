Where it started

Miraculous Moments · Khoảnh Khắc Diệu Kỳ

A story before the film.

Miraculous Moments began with a volunteering experience where I met pediatric cancer survivors, listened to their stories, and shared moments of creativity and joy. Through photography, the children were not simply subjects of a story — they were making images of their own.

01 / Learning together 02 / Taking the camera outside 03 / Graduation day
Before seeing their illness, I wanted people to see the children.

That experience stayed with me. It later shaped The Window Without a Horizon and made me think more seriously about the responsibility of communication: not only how we tell a story, but how we choose to see the person inside it.

Flying Heart

Volunteer work · Community outreach

Field notes from the road.

Flying Heart is part of my journey as a volunteer. These trips brought me closer to community work on the ground, from meeting families and children to helping prepare and distribute gifts. I keep them here not as achievements, but as lived moments that continue shaping how I think about care, presence, and social impact.

Đắk Lắk · Outreach trip

Meeting people where they are.

This trip to Đắk Lắk carried the spirit of a field journal: small encounters, long roads, shared effort, and the quiet reality behind community outreach. The photos move between preparation, presence, and the people we met along the way.

Đồng Nai · Mid-Autumn outreach

A crowded night of celebration.

The Đồng Nai trip felt different: fuller, louder, and more festive. With Mid-Autumn activities, gifts, and a large gathering of children, the focus shifted toward creating a shared moment of joy while still holding onto the values of care and connection.

06

Chạm Việt Phục

Cultural project · Workshop & exhibition

Bringing heritage closer.

Chạm Việt Phục was a student-led cultural project that invited people to engage with Vietnamese traditional clothing in a more approachable way. I joined as part of the organizing effort and contributed through concept support, content direction, and on-site interaction during the event.

Culture becomes more meaningful when people can step into it.

What mattered most to me was not only presenting cultural artifacts, but creating a space where people could ask, listen, and participate. That experience strengthened my interest in cultural communication and in designing activities that help heritage feel lived, shared, and relevant to young audiences.

07

Tiên Thường Tuần Du

Cultural project · Community participation

Tradition in motion.

Tiên Thường Tuần Du was another cultural space where I took part as a volunteer, supporting activities that brought together performance, costume, and community interaction. What drew me to this project was how culture could be experienced not as something distant, but as something alive, shared, and carried by people.

Projects like this taught me that heritage becomes stronger when it is practiced together.

Beyond documentation, I was interested in the atmosphere these events created: people meeting across regions, identities, and traditions. As a volunteer, I saw how cultural projects can become spaces for participation, conversation, and connection — and that is a direction I hope to keep exploring in my work.
